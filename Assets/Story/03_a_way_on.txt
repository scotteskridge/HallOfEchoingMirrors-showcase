A Way On

I have come down this stretch three times.

Each time I was looking for Roland in the glass. That was the error, and I would like it recorded that I found it myself. The chair in the third frame is a little further back on every pass. I have decided not to think about that yet.

So I stop in front of a frame no different from its neighbours, and I look at the frame. Oak leaves are carved into the moulding, badly, by someone in a hurry or someone who had never seen an oak. One corner is joined at slightly the wrong angle. There is a thumbprint in the varnish.

*Somebody made this.*

I run my hand along the edge, and my hand keeps going. The glass does not stop where the frame stops. A hand's breadth of silver runs on between the reflections, like a gap between two hung sheets, into a grey I cannot see the end of.

I have spent twenty-one years teaching young women to look at the vessel and not only at what is in it. I have said it aloud perhaps four hundred times.

Either Roland went this way, or something would like me to think so. I cannot tell which, and I am going anyway. The gap leads to A Dark Hall, and I can Travel there now.
