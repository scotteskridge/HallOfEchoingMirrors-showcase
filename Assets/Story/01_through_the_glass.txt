Through the Glass

The mirror should not be able to take him.

I have warded that frame every season for twenty-one years, and in all that time it has never once looked back at me. Now the glass ripples like water struck from underneath. An arm comes through that is not mine and not his, and Roland is gone between one breath and the next.

I do not decide to follow. My hands are on the frame, then the cold has me, and the laboratory is on the wrong side of the glass.

*Twenty-one years of practice, Clara, and the first thing you do is fall in after him.*

The other side is corridors. Frames without end, each holding a room that is almost ours. The bench is swept clean, which it has not been since 1907. The lamp's green shade is unbroken. It is our laboratory as described to someone who has never stood in it.

The gem at my throat has gone hot, all seven facets lit at once. Something in this place is very glad to see me.

Far down the hall, Roland is struggling, one arm locked across his chest. The thing holding him wears his coat and his careful way of standing. It has his face and none of his kindness. I have never been so sure of anything so quickly.

Chasing after them is the only thing I can do.
