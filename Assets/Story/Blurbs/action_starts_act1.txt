## start_chase
# The chase in loop 1. Only while chasing Roland.

I run. There is no other word for it and no time to find one.
I begin chasing, but my good sense has not finished clearing its throat.
He is too far ahead. I start after him anyway.
I run, and the hall lengthens to meet me.
Every instinct I own says slow down. I do not listen to a single one.
I call his name once, then save my breath for running.
His coat goes round a corner. I go round it after him, and there is more corridor.
I have not run like this since Evercrest, and I was twenty then.
The thing in his coat never hurries. It doesn't need to.
I keep him in sight. That is the whole plan, and it is not much of one.
My shoes were not made for this. Neither, frankly, was I.
Roland looks back once. I run faster, and the hall stretches to suit me.
Frame, frame, frame. I stop seeing them and look only at his back.


## start_travel
# Setting off along a way to the next room.

I set off, counting frames so that I have something to count.
I go on. The floor makes no promises and I ask for none.
I choose my way and take it before I can argue with myself.
Onward, then. The hall is never once shorter the second time.
I walk briskly, the way one walks past a house one does not wish to be asked into.
I leave without looking back. Looking back here is expensive.
I go, and the frames turn their faces as I pass. I have decided that they do not.
One foot, then the next. It worked on the stairs at home with a child on each hip.
I take the next way before the Hall can offer me a worse one.
I set off at the pace I keep for trains I intend to catch.
Onward. He has to be somewhere, and standing still has never once found him.
I straighten my collar and go. Nobody is watching, but the mirrors are.
I pick a direction. The Hall will tell me later whether it was the right one.
Off again. My feet know the way better than I do, and complain less.
I walk on, hands in my pockets so they cannot reach for anything.


## start_explore
# Beginning to explore a room.

Looking around, I see nothing at first, which is where every good look begins.
I start at one corner and work outward, the way he taught me to read a cracked stone.
I stop, and take the measure of the place.
Slowly, then. Nothing here rewards haste.
I look around. Nothing at first. Then less than nothing, which is interesting.
I walk the edges before the middle. Edges tell you what a room is for.
I set myself to learn this place. It does not care to be learned.
I let my eyes go where they like for a while. They are better at this than I am.
I stand quite still and let the room show itself. Rooms, like children, confess to the patient.
First the ceiling. Nobody looks at ceilings, which makes them the best place to hide things.
I note the doors, then the frames, then anything that should not be here.
I look the room over the way I would a house before signing for it.
I begin with what I can touch. This place lies to the eye.
I take a slow turn about the room, as though I were at a dull party.
I start where I came in. It is the only part of the room I trust.


## start_search
# Beginning a search.

I get down on my knees, something I only do for a child's lost shoe.
I begin at the skirting and work along it, inch by inch.
I search the way one searches for a dropped needle: slowly, and cursing.
I go through it again, properly this time.
Looking closely, I find nothing at first. I keep looking.
I roll up my sleeves. Somewhere in here is a thing that does not want finding.
I start with the frames nearest the floor. Things fall; they rarely fall upward.
I search in rows, left to right, as if reading a page someone smudged.
I run my fingers along the first frame and begin.
I look for anything out of place, in a place where everything is.
I begin, and promise myself I will not search it twice. I always search it twice.
