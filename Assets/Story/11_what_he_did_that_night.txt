What He Did That Night

Twenty-one years of remembering this night, and I never once looked at him. I looked at the stone.

So I look now. Sleeves rolled, tongue between his teeth over the third line, entirely happy. It is a strange thing to be jealous of a memory of my own husband.

He does not glance at the corner of the bench, and he should. I remember a blue box there, and in it the white sapphire earrings he bought me and meant to give me that night. I remember it because I remember not looking at it.

They are gone from the bench, and gone from the memory. Someone or something has edited this, and it has been careful.

Watching gets easier with practice: each time I watch, *Watch him* costs me less. Watch him closely enough, and I can *Talk to Roland*.
