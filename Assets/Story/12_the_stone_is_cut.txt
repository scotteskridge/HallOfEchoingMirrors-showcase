The last facet comes away clean.

I have done this a thousand times since that night, in better light, with better tools, for better-paying clients. I have never once done it with my hands so steady.

In the memory, the girl I was lets out her breath. Roland laughs, softly, the way he laughed when a thing went right that he had half expected to go wrong, and puts his hand over mine on the bench, and for one moment the chair beside him is not empty.

The stone in the jaw is warm, and clear, and it holds the light the way a cupped hand holds water.

It is not a memory any longer. It is a thing I am holding.
