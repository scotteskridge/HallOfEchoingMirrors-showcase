Through the Glass, Home

The glass is warm, and it lets me through, and I am standing in my own laboratory with the children's drawings over the sink.

The children. I am at the door before the thought is finished, counting: school lets out at four, and I have been gone for what feels like days.

The clock on the mantel says twenty past ten. It said twenty past ten when the mirror took him. The bread I left rising has not risen.

No time has passed here. None at all.

I stand in the doorway until my heart slows. Then I go back to the mirror, because Roland is still on the other side of it, and nobody else is going to fetch him. From now on I step back in at the Smoky Mirror.
