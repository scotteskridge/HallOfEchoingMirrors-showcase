He Takes the Ring

He looks up at last, and he sees his student. He is waiting for a girl of twenty to say something clever about the facets.

So I tell him what that girl knew, and then what she couldn't have. The blue box at the corner of the bench. The white sapphires in it. That he means to give them to me tonight, and has lost his nerve twice already.

He goes very still. Then he looks at my hands, and my face, and the twenty-one years on it, and I watch him do the sum.

"It's you," he says. It isn't a question.

I hold out the ring with its empty setting. He takes it, turns it once on his finger the way he always does, and the room lets out its breath.

The memory has stopped fighting me. I no longer need the ring to come in, and I can *Search* the Mirror's Laboratory afresh: something has come back to the bench.
