# Hall of Echoing Mirrors — Vision

> What the game is for. One page; it changes rarely, and only when Scott says so. Every feature, in `/design` and `/plan-feature`, names the pillar it serves; one that fights a pillar is flagged before it is built. The detail lives in `GDD.md`.

**Status:** Approved by Scott, 2026-10-01 (pillar 1 in Scott's words; 2–6 drafted by Claude from GDD §2, §3, §14 and §12a, and approved).

## The game in one breath

A story-driven loop game, played actively rather than idly. In 1907 Clara, a practised eromancer, follows her reflection into the Hall of Echoing Mirrors, where her husband Roland is trapped. The hall takes her magic on screen in the first minutes; each run she goes back in, a little emptier, and wins it back piece by piece. A run ends when her vitality runs out; what she has learned, and what she has changed in the hall, stays.

## The player's fantasy

Getting good at a place that is trying to consume you. The pleasure is the run that goes further than the last because *you* planned it better, and the moment you realise how something in the hall works.

## Pillars

1. **Fun comes from agency: meaningful choices.** The player's decisions change how the game plays, and the choices are real ones: never just an obviously optimal answer, and optimisation never collapses to one. (Scott, 2026-10-01; GDD §14.3, §14.4, §14.5, §14.15)
2. **Capability, never patience.** No wall falls to repetition alone. Progress comes from routing, allocation and knowing the hall, so a patient player with a bad plan does not eventually win. (GDD §2.1, §14.2, §14.7)
3. **Every run teaches something.** Fun is learning a pattern. Each run delivers at least one payoff (a new room or action, a lasting unlock, a visibly faster chain, a story passage), a failed run can be diagnosed, and each act authors at least one *aha*. (GDD §14.12, §14.13, §14.14, §14.20)
4. **The hall remembers.** The player builds the world across runs, not only Clara: switches, knowledge and rooms known by heart persist, and the plan itself carries over. (GDD §2.3, §11)
5. **Recovery, not acquisition.** What she wins back was taken from her on screen. The genre's unlock ladder carries the plot, and longing is the reason to go back in. (GDD §2.4, §5, §12a)
6. **Prose is a feature, not the engine.** Clara's voice (first person, present tense) is where this game beats its genre, but the loop must be fun with placeholder text first. (GDD §2.5, §14.8, §14.17)

## Who it's for

Players of *Idle Loops*, *Increlution* and *Stuck in Time* who want an active, readable loop with a story worth finishing; and players who liked the idea of those games but bounced off their bare look. PC, sold on Steam.

## What it is not

- Not idle: fun at default speed, with no speed hacks needed (GDD §3).
- Not a verb-puzzle adventure: hues are schools of magic with thresholds, not *King's Quest* verbs (GDD §14.1).
- Not long for its own sake: length is an output of how much fun content exists, never a target to pad toward (GDD §14.18).
- Not explicit: longing and devotion; romance implied; lust is not a mechanic (GDD §3).
- Tabled for now: the Shadow, Clara's rival reflection, combination magic, multiple endings.

## What Act I must prove

At the end of Act I the player can answer *what do I wish I had more of?* If the answer is **time**, the loop works (GDD §14 *Act I: what it should ask of the player*). Stage 1 is done when a friend has played Act I cold and Scott has judged whether the loop is fun.
